Low Earth Orbit (LEO)

The primary operational domain for SpaceX. Operating near Earth allows for the cheapest access to space, heavy payloads, and ultra-low communication latency (vital for Starlink, deployed at approximately 530-550 km). Objects here experience slight atmospheric drag, meaning defunct satellites eventually burn up harmlessly in the atmosphere, keeping the orbit clean. The ISS, Crew Dragon, and Starlink all operate here.

Medium Earth Orbit (MEO)

Located above LEO but below GEO (2,000 - 35,786 km). It is the sweet spot for global navigation satellite systems (like GPS and Galileo). Satellites here take 2 to 24 hours to circle the Earth.

Geosynchronous Orbit (GEO)

At precisely 35,786 km, satellites match Earth's rotation perfectly, appearing "parked" over a single spot on the equator. Used for traditional TV and weather satellites, but suffers from high ping (latency).
